I started using time-tracking in 2020, when I started working remotely full-time.  
At first, it helped me stay focused, but after a while I mostly used it to record how much time I spent on various categories of work: infrastructure, feature development, debugging, meetings, etc.
It worked because my time is limited and my brain is (mostly) single-threaded, so as an individual contributor, it is generally easy to assign an hour of work to a specific task.  
Not so much anymore.

Since AI agents came along, I can (and do) spin multiple plates at once, and I'm not the one writing code. Time tracking stops being a relevant metric for almost anything. It doesn't capture cost nor complexity anymore.

So, I just stopped doing it.
